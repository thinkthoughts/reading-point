Z — Reading Point

Reading Points and Galerkin Limits

Specification Leads Construction
A · AbstractB · Introduction C · SpecificationD · Galerkin E · CompactnessF · Limit G · GrammarH · Discussion R · References

A — Abstract

We study the specification structure of the Galerkin construction formalized by Wang for global Leray–Hopf weak solutions of the two-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations. The construction begins with finite-dimensional Galerkin systems, where transport cancellation yields an exact energy identity, and proceeds through uniform estimates, space–time compactness, and convergence to a weakly continuous velocity path satisfying the energy inequality at every time. We call each specified time \(t\) a reading point: a point where the established weakly continuous representative and its admissible energy bound can be read directly, while the approximating sequence remains part of the construction that establishes them. The passage from exact finite-dimensional identities to a limiting inequality provides a concrete example of specification leading construction. We formulate this ordering as a specification grammar: leading constraints organize engineering objects and measurable states; leading specifications constrain admissible generalizations; and admissible generalizations trail leading specifications.

B — Introduction: A Reading Point and Its Construction

Consider the normalized, unforced incompressible Navier–Stokes system on a bounded open domain \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2\),

\[ \partial_tu-\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=0,\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0,\qquad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\qquad u(0)=u_0. \tag{1} \]

Wang formalizes in Lean 4 the Galerkin construction of a global Leray–Hopf weak solution for every initial datum in the corresponding solenoidal \(L^2\) velocity space. The resulting solution has a weakly continuous representative

\[ u_c:[0,\infty)\to H_\Omega, \tag{2} \]

satisfies the weak equation on every finite horizon, and obeys the energy inequality at every time.

For every integer \(N\geq0\) and \(t\in[0,N]\),

\[ \|u_c(t)\|_{H_\Omega}^2+ 2\int_0^t \|G_\Omega u_V^N(s)\|_{L^2(Q)}^2\,ds \leq \|u_0\|_{H_\Omega}^2. \tag{3} \]

We call the specified \(t\) a reading point. Once the representative \(u_c\) has been established, its velocity value and admissible energy bound can be read directly at \(t\). The Galerkin approximations remain part of the construction establishing that reading.

At each Galerkin level, the corresponding energy relation is exact. In schematic notation,

\[ E_k(t)+D_k[a,t]=E_k(a). \tag{4} \]

At the limiting reading point,

\[ E(t)+D[a,t]\leq E(a). \tag{5} \]

Weak lower semicontinuity carries the exact approximating balance into the admissible limiting bound.

\[ \boxed{\text{leading specification}\longrightarrow \text{constraints on admissible construction}.} \tag{6} \]

In proof order, the construction produces the objects satisfying that specification. Our contribution is an interpretation of this ordering in Wang’s kernel-checked construction.

C — Leading Specification: The Weak Solution

Choose an auxiliary rectangle \(Q\) satisfying

\[\Omega\subset Q^\circ. \tag{7}\]

Let \(D_\sigma(\Omega)\) denote the compactly supported \(C^1\) divergence-free fields in \(\Omega\), extended by zero to \(Q\). Wang defines \(H_\Omega\) as their closure in \(L^2(Q;\mathbb R^2)\) and \(V_\Omega\) as the closure of their velocity–gradient graphs. The coordinate maps are

\[ J_\Omega:V_\Omega\to H_\Omega,\qquad G_\Omega:V_\Omega\to L^2(Q;\mathbb R^{2\times2}). \tag{8} \]

For every \(u_0\in H_\Omega\), Wang constructs \(u_c\) as in (2) and, for every integer \(N\geq0\),

\[u_V^N\in L^2([0,N];V_\Omega), \tag{9}\]

with

\[ u_c(0)=u_0,\qquad J_\Omega u_V^N=u_c\quad\text{a.e. on }[0,N]. \tag{10} \]

For every \(y\in H_\Omega\),

\[ t\longmapsto\langle u_c(t),y\rangle_{H_\Omega} \tag{11} \]

is continuous.

For \(t\in[0,N]\), define

\[ E(t):=\|u_c(t)\|_{H_\Omega}^2 \tag{12} \]

and

\[ D_N[0,t]:= 2\int_0^t\|G_\Omega u_V^N(s)\|_{L^2(Q)}^2\,ds. \tag{13} \]
\[ \boxed{E(t)+D_N[0,t]\leq E(0).} \tag{14} \]

For every \(\varphi\in V_\Omega\) and scalar time test \(\eta\in C^1([0,N])\) satisfying \(\eta(N)=0\),

\[ \begin{aligned} -&\int_0^N\langle u_c(t),J_\Omega\varphi\rangle_{H_\Omega}\eta'(t)\,dt\\ &+\int_0^N\langle G_\Omega u_V^N(t),G_\Omega\varphi\rangle_{L^2(Q)}\eta(t)\,dt\\ &+\int_0^N b_\Omega(u_V^N(t),u_V^N(t),\varphi)\eta(t)\,dt\\ &=\langle u_0,J_\Omega\varphi\rangle_{H_\Omega}\eta(0). \end{aligned}\tag{15} \]

In our terminology, the initial value, weak continuity, almost-everywhere compatibility, weak equation, and every-time energy inequality form the leading specification. They state what an admissible construction must establish.

D — Finite-Dimensional Galerkin Construction

The finite-horizon construction is carried out on

\[I=[a,b] \tag{16}\]

using the rectangular spaces \(V_Q\) and \(H_Q\). The compact, injective, dense embedding

\[J_Q:V_Q\to H_Q \tag{17}\]

provides spectral coordinates through the compact positive operator

\[T=J_Q^\ast J_Q. \tag{18}\]

There are orthonormal bases \((e_i)\) of \(V_Q\) and \((f_i)\) of \(H_Q\), together with singular values \(\sigma_i>0\), satisfying

\[J_Qe_i=\sigma_i f_i. \tag{19}\]

Nested finite index sets \(K_m\) define

\[H_m=\operatorname{span}\{f_i:i\in K_m\}. \tag{20}\]

The associated orthogonal projections satisfy

\[ J_QP_m^V=P_m^HJ_Q \tag{21} \]

and

\[ \|J_Q-P_m^HJ_Q\|_{V_Q\to H_Q}\to0. \tag{22} \]

Wang constructs exact lifts

\[ E_m:H_m\to V_Q,\qquad J_QE_m=\operatorname{id}_{H_m}, \tag{23} \]

and test projections \(Q_m:V_Q\to H_m\) with

\[ E_mQ_m=P_m^V,\qquad P_m^Vv\to v\quad\text{in }V_Q. \tag{24} \]

These spectral objects connect the \(H_Q\)-coordinates used by the coefficient ODE with the \(V_Q\)-coordinates carrying gradient control.

The convection form satisfies the skew identity

\[ b_Q(u,v,w)=-b_Q(u,w,v), \tag{25} \]

hence

\[b_Q(u,u,u)=0. \tag{26}\]

Riesz representation converts diffusion, convection, and forcing on each \(H_m\) into a coefficient ODE. Abstractly,

\[ \langle R(t,u),v\rangle_H=-a(u,v)-b(u,u,v)+F(t)(v). \tag{27} \]

Wang’s finite-dimensional theorem gives the exact energy identity

\[ \|u(T)\|_H^2+ 2\int_{t_0}^{T}a(u,u)\,dt = \|u_0\|_H^2+ 2\int_{t_0}^{T}F(t)(u(t))\,dt. \tag{28} \]

For the unforced construction \(F=0\),

\[ \boxed{ \|u_m(t)\|_H^2+ 2\int_a^t a(u_m(s),u_m(s))\,ds = \|u_m(a)\|_H^2. } \tag{29} \]

Skew cancellation therefore yields both an exact balance and the uniform control required to continue the coefficient solution across the full interval. The Ladyzhenskaya estimate additionally supplies the uniform dual-space derivative control used in compactness.

\[ \boxed{ J_Q\to\text{spectral coordinates}\to(H_m,E_m,Q_m) \to\text{coefficient ODE}\to\text{exact energy identity}\to\text{uniform bounds}. } \tag{30} \]

E — Compactness and the Reading Point

The uniform bounds prepare the passage to the limit. Each fixed finite-dimensional projection has a uniform \(1/2\)-Hölder modulus in time. Finite-dimensional Arzelà–Ascoli therefore supplies uniform convergence along a subsequence, while (22) controls the high-mode remainder.

The resulting spectral compactness argument yields

\[ u_{H,k}\to u_H\quad\text{strongly in }L^2(I;H_Q) \tag{31} \]

and, along the same selected sequence,

\[ u_{V,k}\rightharpoonup u_V\quad\text{weakly in }L^2(I;V_Q), \tag{32} \]

with

\[J_Qu_V=u_H. \tag{33}\]

The gradient coordinate also converges weakly:

\[ G_Qu_{V,k}\rightharpoonup G_Qu_V \tag{34} \]

in

\[ L^2\!\left(I;L^2(Q;\mathbb R^{2\times2})\right). \tag{35} \]

Wang then uses uniform convergence of every fixed finite-dimensional projection to construct

\[u_c:I\to H_Q \tag{36}\]

such that, for every \(t\in I\),

\[ u_{H,k}(t)\rightharpoonup u_c(t)\quad\text{in }H_Q. \tag{37} \]

For every \(y\in H_Q\),

\[ t\longmapsto\langle u_c(t),y\rangle_{H_Q} \tag{38} \]

is continuous, while

\[ u_c=u_H\quad\text{a.e. on }I \tag{39} \]

and

\[u_c(a)=u_0. \tag{40}\]

Thus the Bochner limit supplies the almost-everywhere class, and the fixed-mode construction lifts that class to a weakly continuous representative specified at every \(t\).

\[ \boxed{t\longmapsto u_c(t)} \tag{41} \]

supplies the reading points.

Ladyzhenskaya interpolation further yields the strong velocity convergence required to pass the quadratic convection term. The same selected subsequence therefore supports the nonlinear limit, the weakly continuous representative, and the energy argument.

F — Exact Identity to Limit Inequality

Fix a reading point

\[t\in I=[a,b]. \tag{42}\]

Let \(R_t\) denote restriction of a time-dependent gradient to \([a,t]\). Define

\[ X_k(t)=\left(u_{H,k}(t),\sqrt2\,R_tG_Qu_{V,k}\right) \tag{43} \]

in the Hilbert direct sum

\[ \mathcal H_t= H_Q\oplus_2 L^2\!\left([a,t];L^2(Q;\mathbb R^{2\times2})\right). \tag{44} \]

Its squared norm is

\[ \|X_k(t)\|_{\mathcal H_t}^2 = \|u_{H,k}(t)\|_{H_Q}^2+ 2\int_a^t\|G_Qu_{V,k}(s)\|_{L^2(Q)}^2\,ds. \tag{45} \]

For the unforced system, the exact Galerkin identity gives

\[ \|X_k(t)\|_{\mathcal H_t}^2 = \|P_{m_k}^Hu_0\|_{H_Q}^2. \tag{46} \]

Orthogonal projection gives

\[ \|P_{m_k}^Hu_0\|_{H_Q}\leq\|u_0\|_{H_Q}. \tag{47} \]

Meanwhile,

\[ X_k(t)\rightharpoonup X(t)\quad\text{in }\mathcal H_t, \tag{48} \]

where

\[ X(t)=\left(u_c(t),\sqrt2\,R_tG_Qu_V\right). \tag{49} \]

The first coordinate converges by (37). The second follows from (34) and boundedness of \(R_t\). Weak lower semicontinuity gives

\[ \|X(t)\|_{\mathcal H_t} \leq \liminf_{k\to\infty}\|X_k(t)\|_{\mathcal H_t}. \tag{50} \]

Since the initial projections converge strongly,

\[ P_{m_k}^Hu_0\to u_0\quad\text{in }H_Q, \tag{51} \]

we obtain

\[ \boxed{ \|u_c(t)\|_{H_Q}^2+ 2\int_a^t\|G_Qu_V(s)\|_{L^2(Q)}^2\,ds \leq \|u_0\|_{H_Q}^2. } \tag{52} \]

The choice of \(t\) was arbitrary, so (52) holds at every time.

\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c} \displaystyle \|u_{H,k}(t)\|_{H_Q}^2+ 2\int_a^t\|G_Qu_{V,k}(s)\|_{L^2(Q)}^2\,ds = \|P_{m_k}^Hu_0\|_{H_Q}^2 \\[4mm] \Downarrow\quad\text{weak convergence + lower semicontinuity} \\[4mm] \displaystyle \|u_c(t)\|_{H_Q}^2+ 2\int_a^t\|G_Qu_V(s)\|_{L^2(Q)}^2\,ds \leq \|u_0\|_{H_Q}^2. \end{array} } \tag{53} \]

The equality records the exact finite-dimensional balance. The inequality records the admissible property carried to the limiting reading point.

For the bounded open domain \(\Omega\), Wang applies the finite-horizon construction on every \([0,N]\). Galerkin paths are compatible on overlapping horizons, and uniform limits of their fixed projections identify the weakly continuous representatives pointwise on those overlaps. One global path follows:

\[ u_c:[0,\infty)\to H_\Omega. \tag{54} \]

Its restriction to every \([0,N]\) carries the weak equation and every-time energy inequality.

G — Specification Grammar

The Galerkin argument now displays two compatible orders.

The construction order records how the result is established:

\[ \boxed{ \text{Galerkin spaces}\to\text{ODE}\to\text{exact identity}\to \text{uniform bounds}\to\text{compactness}\to\text{limit}\to u_c(t)\to\text{energy inequality}. } \tag{55} \]

The specification order records what an admissible construction must establish:

\[ \boxed{\mathcal S\longrightarrow\text{constraints on admissible }\mathcal C.} \tag{56} \]

Here \(\mathcal S\) contains the initial value, weak continuity, compatibility of the velocity and energy representatives, weak equation, and every-time energy inequality.

A specification is leading where it constrains what counts as an admissible construction or generalization. “Leading” therefore specifies an ordering of constraints rather than a chronology of proof steps.

The construction introduces engineering objects including

\[ J_Q,\quad(e_i,f_i,\sigma_i),\quad H_m,\quad E_m,\quad Q_m,\quad P_m^H,\quad P_m^V,\quad u_m,\quad R_t. \tag{57} \]

These objects mediate between the leading constraints and the limiting quantities established by the proof.

At a reading point, the relevant measurable state is

\[ X(t)=\left(u_c(t),\sqrt2\,R_tG_Qu_V\right), \tag{58} \]

with

\[ \|X(t)\|_{\mathcal H_t}^2 = \|u_c(t)\|_{H_Q}^2+ 2\int_a^t\|G_Qu_V(s)\|_{L^2(Q)}^2\,ds. \tag{59} \]
\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c} \textbf{Leading constraints}\\ \downarrow\\ \textbf{Engineering objects}\\ \downarrow\\ \textbf{Measurable states}\\[2mm] \textbf{Leading specifications constrain admissible generalizations}\\ \textbf{Admissible generalizations trail leading specifications}. \end{array} } \tag{60} \]

If \(\mathcal C'\) denotes a proposed generalization, its admissibility relative to the specification \(\mathcal S\) can be written schematically as

\[\mathcal C'\vdash\mathcal S. \tag{61}\]

The criterion concerns preservation of specification. Alternative approximation machinery can qualify where it establishes the specified properties.

\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{lll} \textbf{Specification order:}&\mathcal S&\longrightarrow\text{constraints on }\mathcal C,\\[2mm] \textbf{Construction order:}&\mathcal C&\longrightarrow X(t)\longrightarrow\mathcal S_t. \end{array} } \tag{62} \]

The first line specifies admissibility. The second establishes it.

\[ \boxed{\textbf{Specification leads construction; construction establishes the reading point.}} \tag{63} \]

H — Discussion: Specification Leads Construction

Wang’s formalization exposes a detailed construction: graph spaces, compact spectral coordinates, projections, finite-dimensional ODEs, transport cancellation, exact energy identities, uniform estimates, space–time compactness, nonlinear convergence, a weakly continuous representative, and weak lower semicontinuity. The theorem presents the result through a smaller collection of properties of the established limiting solution.

The reading point identifies one such state:

\[ X(t)=\left(u_c(t),\sqrt2\,R_tG_Qu_V\right),\qquad \|X(t)\|_{\mathcal H_t}^2\leq\|u_0\|_{H_Q}^2. \tag{64} \]

The approximating sequence establishes \(u_c\); the established representative supplies \(u_c(t)\) at every specified \(t\). The same selected subsequence supports the strong velocity limit, weak energy-space limit, weakly continuous representative, nonlinear passage, and energy inequality.

\[ \boxed{\text{exact approximating identity}\longrightarrow \text{admissible limiting inequality}.} \tag{65} \]

Here the transition from \(=\) to \(\leq\) is carried by weak convergence and weak lower semicontinuity. The target property remains the every-time energy bound specified for the Leray–Hopf solution.

The global construction adds another lift. Finite-horizon representatives agree pointwise on overlaps, so a specified \(t\geq0\) can be read through any integer horizon containing it. Wang thereby obtains

\[ \boxed{t\in[0,\infty)\longmapsto u_c(t)\in H_\Omega.} \tag{66} \]

The restriction of this path to every finite horizon retains the initial value, weak continuity, weak equation, and every-time energy inequality.

The specification grammar extracted from this example is

\[ \boxed{\text{leading constraints}\to\text{engineering objects}\to\text{measurable states},} \tag{67} \]

together with

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Leading specifications constrain admissible generalizations.}\\ \text{Admissible generalizations trail leading specifications.} \end{gathered} } \tag{68} \]

For this paper, the grammar is an interpretation of the mathematical structure exposed by Wang’s formalization. Other approximation arguments can be read through their own leading constraints, engineering objects, measurable states, and limiting operations.

\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c} \textbf{Specification leads construction.}\\[2mm] \textbf{Construction establishes the reading point.}\\[2mm] \textbf{The reading point carries the admissible specification.} \end{array} } \tag{69} \]
